## Hypothesis record (H-R4-E2)

- **Claim:** Nested Rollout Policy Adaptation (NRPA, Rosin 2011 / Cazenave) finds good constructions that greedy or local search misses. It builds a whole Cayley colouring one decision at a time and scores only completed objects.
- **Prediction:** With the same number of completed evaluations, NRPA beats random rollouts and greedy bit-flip descent. It would reach a new basin below 0.030139.
- **Disconfirmation:** Any of these counts against it: NRPA does no better than the baselines; NRPA plateaus far above the incumbent; or its best point is a local optimum that a seeded search around it cannot improve.

## Domain and evaluator

- **Group:** G = Z_m x F_{2^k}. One decision fixes the red/blue colour of one orbit of G\{0}. The orbits come from the group H generated by negation, a multiplier of order dZ on Z_m (the group must contain -1), multiplication by the subgroup of order dF in F_{2^k}^*, and optionally Frobenius.
- **Objective:** t(K4,W)+t(K4,1-W) of the 0/1 blow-up. Red has no loops and the diagonal is blue, so repeated indices count, which matches the repo convention.
- **Exact count:** hom = n * sum_a f(a), where f(a) counts the pairs (b,c) that close a monochromatic K4 on (0,a,b,c). The sum is taken over H-orbit representatives, since f is H-invariant. It uses bitset translates and integer arithmetic.
- **Oracle:** 100/100 random colourings on 5 tiny groups matched a brute-force O(n^4) count (Z3xF4, F16, Z13, Z3xF4 with Frobenius, Z5xF2). Output is in `reports/round4-E2-oracle-001/oracle.txt`.
- **Independent check:** the compiled Lean `check_candidate` recounted the best graph and agreed exactly: 10625442816/768^4 (`reports/round4-E2-leancheck-001`).
- **Search modes (matched budget of completed evaluations):**
  - **NRPA:** context-free binary codes, alpha = 1, restarted at the top level until the budget runs out.
  - **Random:** uniform rollouts.
  - **Greedy:** first-improvement single-flip descent from random starts, with restarts.
  - **Seeded:** NRPA with its policy biased toward a seed colouring, plus descent.

### About the dF=3 attractor (the overall best, from dF=5, is in the batch F/G table above)

- **Value:** 0.030542329506 exactly 10625442816/768^4. The Lean recount agrees.
- **Structure:** red degree 386, 84 of 171 orbits red. The L3 run (seed 1, 60k evaluations) and the L4 run (160k) hit the same exact value. Their colour sequences differ in 86 of 171 positions, so they are probably the same graph up to automorphism. That points to a strong attractor.
- **Local optimality:** the graph is a strict single-flip local minimum in the full 511-decision inverse-pair space. NRPA seeded around it found nothing better.
- **Gap:** it sits 1.34% above the incumbent 0.0301389 and 1.3% above the published 0.0301449 (on the same group order, 768).

## Decision

- **H-R4-E2, relative to the baselines: supported (restricted positive).** With a matched budget of completed evaluations, NRPA beat random and greedy in 9 of 11 small-budget comparisons. It beat them decisively at level 3 in the 171-decision family: 0.03054 against 0.03143 for greedy and 0.03153 for random, and this replicated on a second seed. Levels 3 and 4 in the families dF=3, dF=5 and F256 dF=3 converge repeatedly to a few isolated attractors near 0.03054–0.03076. The advantage grows with nesting level, which fits the claim that sequential construction finds structures that single-flip descent misses.
- **H-R4-E2, absolute (a new basin below 0.030139): not reached; revise.** In every tested family the NRPA optimum is about 1.3% above the incumbent. The best is 0.0305343 against 0.0301389. The best basin is the single-flip local minimum described above. This is a restricted negative for these orbit families, budgets and context-free codes. It is not a statement about Cayley graphs in general: the published 0.0301449 graph is itself a Z3xZ2^8 Cayley graph, just not invariant under our chosen H.
- **No candidate below 0.03014, so nothing is submitted for promotion.**

## Next test

1. Match the published graph's symmetry. Compute Aut of the PPSS 768 graph, take its translation subgroup and point stabiliser, and pick orbit families that contain it (it is not invariant under our order-3 Singer subgroup). Then run NRPA at level 3–4 in the family of ~100–200 orbits that contains the published point. The claim to test is that NRPA re-finds 0.030145 from scratch. If it cannot, it cannot open a new basin at that scale either.
2. Use context-dependent codes, keyed on (decision, colour, colour of a structurally related orbit such as the image under x -> 2x). The current codes are independent per decision, so the policy is a product distribution close to the cross-entropy method.
3. Use the d3 attractor as a seed for relation-engine fractional polishing. This is only worth doing if item 1 shows the family can reach the 0.03014 level.
