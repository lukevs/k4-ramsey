# Round 4 lane E7 — symmetry-projected Cayley LNS on new groups (order 512–2048)

Lane owner: E7 (exploration). Window 22:13–23:12 UTC 2026-09-27. Local, one
single-threaded process at a time, every job under `timeout 600`.

## Hypothesis record (H-R4-E7)

- **Claim.** Cayley graphs of groups not yet tried, of order 512–2048, with the
  orbits of generators under an automorphism subgroup A as variables, contain a
  basin that, after fractional polishing, differs from the incumbent family and
  gets under 0.030139 (target: below 0.030138).
- **Prediction.** An exact block large-neighbourhood search (LNS) finds 0/1
  seeds near the PPSS value (about 0.03015) and does better than single-flip
  descent given the same time. Fractional polishing of those seeds drops below
  0.03014.
- **Disconfirmation.** Every new group/A family plateaus above 0.03014 after
  polishing, or the only good basin reproduces the known about 0.030139 level
  with no gain.

## Method (code in `experiments/round4_E7/`)

- `gen.py p m h f out [r]`: the group G = (F_{p^m}, +) x Z_r. A is generated by
  three maps: scalar multiplication by the order-h subgroup of F^*, the
  Frobenius x -> x^(p^f) (only if f > 0), and negation on each factor. It writes
  the orbit ids and the difference table y - x.
- `cay.cpp` (ids fit in 8 bits, so K <= 255) and `cay9.cpp` (9-bit ids,
  K <= 511). For each orbit representative a, and all b and c, the code records
  the sorted 6-multiset of orbit ids of (a, b, c, a^-1 b, a^-1 c, b^-1 c),
  weighted by the orbit size. This builds the exact orbit polynomial
  F = sum_m c_m (prod p + prod (1 - p)), where the identity orbit has p = 0,
  and density = F / n^3 (blue diagonal convention).
  - **LNS:** one random start, then steepest single-flip descent. After that it
    repeatedly frees 18 random orbit variables and finds the exact optimum over
    all 2^18 settings of the free block. The free-block polynomial is
    restricted, then a subset zeta transform gives the red total at S and the
    blue total at the complement of S. Improvements are accepted.
  - **flip:** steepest single-flip descent with random restarts, given the same
    time.
  - **frac:** projected Adam, then backtracking projected gradient. The result
    is rounded to q = 65536 (or 32768 when n > 1024) and evaluated exactly with
    unsigned 128-bit integers.
- `lift.py`: moves orbit probabilities from a coarse A to a finer A on the same
  table. This is the symmetry-hierarchy continuation.
- `materialize.py`: writes the `rational-step-graphon-v1` JSON, its SHA-256 and
  the predicted fraction. `floatcheck.py` recomputes the density in floating
  point, independently of the polynomial, from rows 0, 5 and 400.

## Independence from B192

**NO (strong evidence; a formal isomorphism test of the connection sets was not
run).** Evidence from `experiments/round4_E7/indep.py`:

- The kernel has a trivial translation-period group, so it is not literally a
  blow-up.
- Averaging over the Z_4 factor ({0, 3, 6, 9} in Z_12) gives a 192 kernel at
  0.031376. That is the wrong quotient.
- Quotienting by the order-4 subgroup H = <(x = 1, t = 0), (x = 56, t = 6)> ≅
  Z_2^2 instead works: 95.3% of H-cosets of G are constant in p. The averaged
  192-block quotient has density **0.03013897744**, which equals B192's
  0.0301389773. Its levels are {0 (92), 1 (87), about 0.779 (12 values in
  0.77888–0.77934), 0.535 (1)}. This is B192's two-level pattern
  (p = 0.779181, h = 0.534265) plus 0/1.
- The 768 kernel has 9 levels: 0 (369), 1 (361), and 7 fractional numerators
  over 65536, namely 27192 ×1, 36553 ×4, 36614 ×8, 40990 ×4, 47530 ×1,
  51071 ×16, 61153 ×4.
- So E7's optimum is a Z_2^2 lift of B192, with symmetry breaking on 4.7% of
  cosets that gains 5.0e-8. This is the same kind of mechanism as d476's
  5-fold lift. The search found it independently: it started from random
  Frobenius-symmetric 0/1 LNS seeds on a different presentation, with no B192
  input. It is therefore an independent *rediscovery route* to the B192 basin,
  not an independent construction.

## Findings

1. **LNS vs flip.** Seed 4 on the K = 98 Z_2^6 x Z_12 family, 90 s each:
   LNS 0.0301696 vs flip 0.0314238. At the same time, exact 18-variable block LNS always
   matched or beat single-flip descent. Examples: 0.0305423 vs 0.0313997 on
   the K = 172 family, and 0.0312076 vs 0.0314004 on F_729. They tied only on
   the tiny K = 18 family. [computed, restricted scope]
2. **Scalar-orbit families on pure field groups plateau near 1/32**, at about
   0.0311–0.0313 even after fractional polishing. Large scalar symmetry is
   harmful. [computed]
3. **The Z_4 x Z_3 cyclic factor on top of Z_2^6 is special.** Z_12 gave about
   0.03015–0.03017. Z_3, Z_16, Z_20, Z_24 and Z_48 all gave 0.0305 or worse.
4. **The good basin is rare for LNS.** Only 1 of 3 seeds reached the 0.03015
   binary basin. The others stalled at 0.03053.
5. **Continuation down the symmetry hierarchy works:** 0.030140289 →
   0.030138928 as the symmetry drops from order 12 to inversion only. The gain
   is almost all in the final symmetry-free step. The result lands at the
   B192 basin (see Independence): it is a lift of B192 with small symmetry
   breaking, not a deeper new basin. [computed]

## Decision

**Revise → retire as a record route.** The mechanism reaches 0.0301389 on
Z_2^6 x Z_4 x Z_3. The optimum turns out to be a symmetry-broken Z_2^2 lift of
B192. It shows no sign of a robust
margin below 0.030138. Retire the pure-field and large-scalar-symmetry
families.
