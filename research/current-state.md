## Exact scope

For a red/blue coloring G of a complete graph, let M4(G) count unordered
four-vertex subsets with all six edges the same color. The parent problem is
to determine

    c4 = lim_{n -> infinity} min_{|V(G)|=n} M4(G) / binom(n,4).

Our construction task is to give an infinite family with limiting density at
or below the hill reference. This is not the problem of constructing graphs
without a red K4 and without a blue Kt, and not a search for R(4,t) bounds.
An improved construction gives an upper bound on c4, not its exact value.

The local [hill specification](../.autolab/hills/clique-cluster-ramsey-multiplicity/README.md)
accepts a symmetric binary matrix A with meaningful diagonal and positive
integer weights w. Block i expands to t*w_i distinct vertices; A_ii selects
the color inside that block. Write Q = sum_i w_i. Its exact limiting density is

    P(A,w) = sum_{i,j,k,l} w_i*w_j*w_k*w_l * I(i,j,k,l) / Q^4,

where I is 1 exactly when the six pair entries of A all agree. Repeated
template indices are included: distinct expanded vertices can belong to the
same block. Ignoring these repetitions computes the wrong objective.

Hill limits are 1..1024 blocks and weights 1..65535. The initial local search
will use the valid subspace of 768 blocks, all weights 1, all diagonal entries
0 (blue). Our own evaluator is permitted; use the hill's mathematical objective,
not its unavailable private audit fixtures, as the specification.

## Directly relevant prior work

Parczyk, Pokutta, Spiegel, and Szabó's
[New Ramsey Multiplicity Bounds and Search Heuristics](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04036v3)
is the primary source. Section 3 searches Cayley generating sets using tabu
search and simulated annealing. Symmetry reduces the number of search variables;
the headline c4 construction has 768 vertices.

The final note records McKay improving that seed by changing edge/non-edge
statuses. His graph has 148724 edges, 536 vertices of degree 387, 232 of degree
388, and trivial automorphism group; the citation is a 2024 personal
communication. We have not obtained its adjacency matrix. Those statistics
do not identify the graph or prove the edit set was a matching.

Section 5.3 reports that the authors' learning-based experiments performed
worse than tabu search and annealing in quality and runtime on medium/large
instances. This is evidence for our computational starting point, not a proof
that all learning-based methods are inferior.

Our inference: start from the existing seed and allow unrestricted edge edits;
do not force the final graph to remain Cayley or regular. Retain symmetry-based
search as a later source of diverse seeds. This follows the successful search
pattern without claiming access to McKay's implementation.

## Exact objective in the initial search space

Let E_b, T_b, K4_b denote unordered blue edges, triangles, and four-cliques
among distinct template vertices; let K4_r denote red four-cliques. For n
unit-weight blocks with blue diagonals,

    N = n + 14*E_b + 36*T_b + 24*(K4_r + K4_b)
    P = N / n^4.

Derivation by multiplicities of template indices in an ordered quadruple:

- One distinct index contributes n.
- Two distinct indices contribute 14 per blue edge: 8 of type 3+1 and 6
  of type 2+2.
- Three distinct indices contribute 36 per blue triangle: three choices of
  repeated index and 12 orderings each.
- Four distinct indices contribute 24 per monochromatic K4.

Red configurations with repeated indices are impossible because the diagonal
is blue. This formula must not be reused unchanged for general weights or
mixed diagonal colors.
